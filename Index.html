Personal Health Behavior Dashboard: Lifestyle Factors Associated with Cervical Pain

Project Story: This dashboard explores how everyday lifestyle behaviors may influence cervical pain patterns using personal health tracking data collected from March to May. The project transforms daily experiences into measurable evidence by examining relationships among physical activity, screen exposure, sleep habits, and household workload. The purpose of this project is to identify behavioral patterns associated with pain fluctuations and support evidence-based personal decision-making. Instead of relying only on memory or subjective feelings, this dashboard uses data visualization to reveal trends that may influence health outcomes.

🔍 Analysis & Strategic Decisions

Key Analytical Insights

Insight 1. Walking Activity and Cervical Pain: 🚶 Walking activity alone does not consistently explain cervical pain fluctuations.

Insight 2. Screen Time and Cervical Pain: 📱 Higher screen exposure appears associated with increased pain scores.

Insight 3. The Sleep Deprivation Link: 🌙 Sleeping after midnight is associated with higher cervical pain outcomes.

Insight 4. Interconnected Lifestyle Factors: ⚡ Multiple lifestyle behaviors interact together rather than acting independently.

Decision-Making Section

Future Actions Based on Data: Based on personal tracking data, future health decisions should focus on improving behavioral consistency rather than changing one factor alone.

Planned Actions:

  • ✓ Maintain regular daily movement: Keep steps up to buffer physical strains.
  • ✓ Reduce prolonged screen exposure: Break up long coding and study blocks to prevent static posture strain.
  • ✓ Prioritize sleeping before midnight: Focus on early rest windows to maximize quality recovery.
  • ✓ Monitor workload balance during high-stress periods: Track physical labor like household chores against study sessions.

⚖️ Accountability, Ethics & Metadata Standards

🔒 Privacy Statement

This dashboard contains only personal behavioral tracking information including step counts, screen exposure duration, sleep timing, household workload intensity, and self-reported pain scores. No identifying personal information, location data, account information, or sensitive private identifiers are included.

⚠️ Limitations and Bias Considerations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting findings: Small personal dataset collected across a limited timeframe Pain scores involve subjective self-reporting Hardware Omission Bias Memory bias may influence manually recorded observations.Correlation does not indicate direct causation. Factors not tracked may influence pain outcomes.
