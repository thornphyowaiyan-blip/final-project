Lifestyle behaviors and Cervical Pain: A dashboard Analysis

Introduction
The lifestyle behaviors which prolonged screen exposure, irregular sleep schedules, reduced physical activity and household labor demands, might influence the musculoskeletal health outcomes. Cervical pain is commonly experienced as neck discomfort or strain. It can cause through the changes of behavioral and environmental factors. By understanding the interaction of these factors, they may help improve personal health decision making.
This project is applied a personal health story into a structured data dashboard by collecting and analyzing lifestyle observations of multiple weeks. The study investigates the relationship among daily step volume, screen interaction duration, sleep timing, sleep duration and physical labor intensity against self-reported cervical pain scores. 
The objective of the dashboard is not to establish medical causation. But it identifies behavioral pattersn that may support healthier future decisions. Six visualizations were developed to explore relationships within the dataset and support evidence to interpret these data. 

Dataset design
The dataset includes personal daily observation recorded from the month of March to May. It includes 13 columns and 79 rows. In the columns, variables are included:
•	Steps
•	Active_time(minutes)
•	Distance_KM
•	Total calories
•	Sleep after midnight (yes = 1 or no =0)
•	Sleep hours
•	Pain scores (0-3 = stable baseline pain, 4-6 = Moderate strain, 7-10 = severe pain)
•	Class hours
•	Study hours
•	Morning walk
•	Heavy chores (yes = 1 or no = 0)
•	Total screen time (class time + study time)
Rows are showing the date of the date in a month. The months started from 1st, March to 18th, May. 
The dashboard combines line trends, correlation analysis, grouped comparisons and interaction heatmap to examine the connection between pain and behavior patterns.

Overall findings
Several major trends occurred in the dashboard.
1.	Sleep timing demonstrated the strongest behavioral relationship because sleeping after midnight consistently produced higher pain scores.
2.	Screen exposure showed a weak positive relationship with pain because higher screen interaction hours caused greater discomfort.
3.	Physical activity volume alone did not predict pain severity because movement patterns remained relatively stable despite substantial pain fluctuations.
4.	Combined behavioral exposures produced stronger effects. Interaction heatmaps showed that multiple lifestyle behaviors occurring together increased pain risk more consistently than individual factors. 
5.	Recovery behaviors may protect against discomfort because earlier sleep timing and balanced workload patterns appeared associated with improved outcomes. 
Decision Making Recommendation
Based on dashboard findings, future behavioral adjustments should reduce prolonged screening time, prioritize sleeping before midnight, maintain consistent movement habits, schedule intensive household labor during better recovery periods and monitor combination of all behavioral factors rather than individual factors alone. These recommendations are supported by observation trends but should not be interpreted as clinical conclusions.
Ethics and Limitations
Several limitations should be considered. 
Privacy Protection 
 The dataset excludes personally identifying information and contains only behavioral observations.
Dataset limitations
Small observational datasets, subjective pain scoring, recall bias, and single person data collection are potential limitations for data interpretation. It also has a limitation of correlation rather than causation. 
Visualization Responsibility
Visualization simplifies interpretation of behavioral data. But it may oversimplify the complex health relationships of cervical spondylosis disease. Behavioral observations should support reflection and informed choices rather than medical diagnosis.
